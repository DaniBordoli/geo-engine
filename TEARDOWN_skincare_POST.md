# We asked ChatGPT the 15 skincare questions shoppers actually ask. 19 of 24 popular brands never came up. Not once.

*Data collected 2026-08-02. Method + full table at the bottom — nothing here is vibes.*

---

## The hook

We took 24 of the most-talked-about skincare brands — The Ordinary, Glossier, Drunk Elephant, COSRX, Paula's Choice, Youth to the People, Starface, Beauty of Joseon and more — and asked ChatGPT the **15 buying questions real shoppers ask** ("best vitamin C serum for dull skin", "best gentle cleanser for sensitive skin", "best sunscreen with no white cast"…).

**19 of the 24 brands were invisible.** Zero mentions across all 15 questions. The AI kept recommending the same tiny handful:

> **The Ordinary was the #1 recommended brand in 19 of 24 answer sets. CeraVe and La Roche-Posay took the rest.**

If a shopper asks ChatGPT what to buy, most of these brands simply don't exist.

## The method (so you can trust it)

- **Engine:** ChatGPT (OpenAI). One engine, on purpose — it's where most people ask.
- **Prompts:** the **same 15** category buying questions for every brand. Not tailored to any brand — that's the whole point, so the numbers are comparable head-to-head.
- **Share-of-voice:** of all the brand names ChatGPT mentioned across those answers, what % were *you*.
- **Invisible:** the % of the 15 questions where your brand never appeared.

No cherry-picking: same questions, same engine, every brand.

## The pattern (this is the part worth sharing)

1. **Buzz ≠ AI visibility.** The most hyped DTC brands on Instagram and TikTok — Glossier, Drunk Elephant, Youth to the People, Starface, Topicals — scored a flat **0%**. A million followers doesn't buy you a single ChatGPT recommendation.
2. **K-beauty darlings vanish too.** COSRX, Beauty of Joseon, Glow Recipe are all over "get ready with me" videos — and land at ~0% when someone asks the AI a plain category question.
3. **The winners have one thing in common:** they're the brands with mountains of *citable, structured, third-party content* — dermatologist write-ups, ingredient explainer pages, "best of" listicles. That's the diet LLMs are trained and grounded on. The Ordinary didn't win on hype; it won on being written about, everywhere, in a machine-readable way.

## Why this matters

Shopping is quietly moving into the chat box. "What should I buy for dark spots?" is becoming a question people ask ChatGPT, not Google. In that world, **a missing mention is a missing sale** — and unlike SEO, there's no page 2 to scroll to. You're either in the answer or you don't exist.

## What to do about it

The fix isn't ads. It's giving the models something to cite: clear, structured, claim-backed content about what your product is for and who it's for — plus the schema and off-site mentions that let an LLM connect the dots. It's very doable, and most brands haven't started.
