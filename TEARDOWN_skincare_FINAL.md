## POST (blog / LinkedIn / dev.to)

**Title:** *I asked ChatGPT what skincare to buy, 15 times. It ignored 19 of 24 top brands.*

**Body:**

Shoppers increasingly ask ChatGPT "what's the best X for Y" instead of Googling.
So I ran a simple test: I took 24 popular skincare brands and asked ChatGPT the
15 category questions real shoppers ask — "best vitamin C serum for dull skin",
"gentle cleanser for sensitive skin", "best retinol for beginners", and so on —
then measured how often each brand got recommended (share-of-voice).

**The result: 19 of the 24 brands were never mentioned once.** Zero. Across all 15
questions. Including some of the biggest, most-hyped DTC and K-beauty names on
TikTok and Instagram — brands with millions of followers landed at a flat 0%.

**The brands ChatGPT actually recommends?** Mostly clinical and drugstore names:
The Ordinary led at ~15% share-of-voice, followed by La Roche-Posay, CeraVe,
Paula's Choice and Neutrogena. Notice the pattern — these aren't the buzzy DTC
darlings. They're the brands with deep, structured, authoritative content that
LLMs trust and cite.

**Three takeaways:**
1. **Social buzz ≠ AI visibility.** A million TikTok followers buys you exactly
   zero ChatGPT recommendations.
2. **Clinical > hype.** The winners read like a dermatologist's list, not a "get
   ready with me" video.
3. **You're probably invisible and don't know it.** None of these brands can see
   this about themselves — there's no dashboard for "what ChatGPT says about you".

That last one is why I built a free scanner: paste your store, it runs the same
kind of test and shows you where you stand. Link below.

*Method: 24 brands, 15 fixed category prompts, ChatGPT, share-of-voice = % of
questions where the brand is recommended. One engine, one snapshot — but a telling
one.*
